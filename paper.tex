\begin{abstract}
We compare general-purpose constraint optimisation and a project attention-based deep Q-learning baseline for three-dimensional hydrophobic--polar lattice folding under a common CPU allocation. Eight benchmark sequences of lengths 20--58 are evaluated with five seeds per method, eight CPU cores per run and an elapsed compute budget of up to 24 hours. The endpoint is the best independently verified complete fold found within the budget, including reinforcement-learning training cost. All 80 observations have valid budget coverage or an applicable declared-domain optimality proof; six observations reuse unchanged seed-0 feasibility runs. The cluster analyser checked 363,488 recorded fold witnesses without verification errors. Both methods reached 11 and 13 contacts on the two shortest sequences in every seed. CP-SAT obtained higher contact counts on all six remaining sequences in every paired seed; its one-hour incumbents also exceeded the corresponding 24-hour reinforcement-learning incumbents. CP-SAT proved optimality within the declared cube for the first three sequences. Longer reinforcement-learning workloads completed only part of their prescribed training schedule. These results demonstrate an advantage for CP-SAT under the tested cold-start CPU budget, rather than superiority over fully trained or GPU-accelerated reinforcement learning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The hydrophobic--polar (HP) lattice model reduces a protein sequence to hydrophobic (H) and polar (P) residues~\cite{Dill1985,Lau1989}. Folding is a self-avoiding walk on a lattice, and the objective is to maximise non-consecutive H--H contacts. This discrete formulation provides a precisely checkable optimisation benchmark, while remaining substantially simpler than physical protein folding. The optimisation problem is NP-hard on the three-dimensional cubic lattice~\cite{BergerLeighton1998}.

Constraint-based approaches encode chain connectivity, self-avoidance and contact formation directly. Specialised tools such as CPSP-tools exploit precomputed hydrophobic cores and constraint satisfaction~\cite{CPSP2009}. Our implementation instead uses general-purpose OR-Tools CP-SAT to encode the complete conformation within a declared coordinate domain~\cite{GoogleORTools,Perron2023}. This supports independently checkable solutions, objective bounds and, when the search closes the gap, domain-scoped optimality proofs. It is not a claim to match the performance of specialised HP solvers.

Attention-based deep Q-learning treats folding as a sequential decision problem~\cite{AttnDRL2504}. Its practical cost includes per-sequence training, model inference and exploratory search. A comparison under a common CPU budget therefore answers a different question from comparing fully trained neural policies with a solver or evaluating GPU training throughput. Both questions are useful, but their costs and endpoints must remain distinct.

We ask which of CP-SAT and a preserved project attention-DQN baseline finds better verified folds within a 24-hour cold-start CPU budget. We evaluate eight fixed sequences with five seeds per method and a common eight-core allocation. Our contribution is this completed, reproducible budget comparison, together with training diagnostics, per-seed trajectories and domain-scoped solver statuses. We retain the project baseline and its limitations rather than silently modifying its learning algorithm after observing feasibility results. A fixed RL--CP-SAT feasibility study and a local learned-repair prototype provide exploratory context in Appendix~\ref{sec:exploratory}; neither is a learned-hybrid arm of the completed comparison.

\section{Problem and Methods}
\subsection{HP Folding and Independent Verification}
The HP protein folding problem on a three-dimensional lattice is defined as follows. A protein sequence is represented as a string:
\begin{equation}
S = s_1 s_2 \ldots s_n,
\end{equation}
where each residue $s_i$ is classified as either hydrophobic (H) or polar (P). A conformation $C$ is a self-avoiding walk on the three-dimensional integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$, meaning each residue is placed at a distinct position:
\begin{equation}
c_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i) \in \mathbb{Z}^3,
\end{equation}
and no two residues occupy the same lattice point. The adjacency constraint requires that consecutive residues along the chain are placed at Manhattan distance 1:
\begin{equation}
||c_i - c_{i+1}||_1 = 1 \quad \forall i \in \{1, \ldots, n-1\}.
\end{equation}
where $||v||_1 = |x| + |y| + |z|$ for a vector $v = (x, y, z)$.

The energy of a conformation is determined by the number of non-consecutive H-H contacts. A contact occurs when two hydrophobic residues are neighbours on the lattice but not adjacent along the sequence. Formally, the energy function is defined as
\begin{equation}
E(C) = -\left|\{(i,j) : i<j, \, s_i = s_j = \text{H}, \, j-i > 1, \, ||c_i - c_j||_1 = 1\}\right|,
\end{equation}
where the set counts all pairs of non-consecutive H-residues that are lattice-adjacent. The objective is to minimise $E(C)$, which is equivalent to maximising the number of favourable hydrophobic contacts. This formulation reflects the simplified physical principle that hydrophobic residues prefer to cluster together, shielding themselves from the surrounding aqueous environment. An illustration of this energy function is provided in Figure~\ref{fig:hp_lattice}.

Dotted magenta lines indicate valid non-consecutive H-H contacts that contribute to the energy function; this conformation has energy $-9\epsilon_{HH}$.}\label{fig:hp_lattice}
\end{figure}


Every reported solution is a complete fold. An independent integer-coordinate checker verifies anchoring, unit backbone bonds, self-avoidance and the contact count without importing the neural model or solver. Individual contact counts are integers; fractional reported scores are averages over sequences or seeds. An absent solution remains missing rather than becoming a zero-contact solution.

\subsection{Compact CP-SAT Model}\label{sec:cpsat_approach}
The primary CPU implementation uses one integer coordinate triple per residue and an injective integer site identifier. Let $R=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. The common domain is $|x_i|,|y_i|,|z_i|\le R$, with the origin and first bond fixed. Reachability additionally bounds each coordinate of zero-based residue $i$ by $i$. An \texttt{AllDifferent} constraint on the site identifiers enforces self-avoidance. Absolute coordinate differences impose unit backbone distance. For each non-consecutive H--H pair of opposite chain parity, a Boolean is true if and only if its Manhattan distance equals one. Maximising their sum therefore maximises the independently recomputed contact count~\cite{GoogleORTools,Perron2023}.

A fresh CP-only run searches the full declared cube without a heuristic hint. A repair fixes all coordinates outside its movable window, retains both boundary bonds and every self-avoidance constraint, and constrains the contact objective not to fall below the incumbent. The incumbent is retained on solver failure. \texttt{OPTIMAL} in a repair refers only to that neighbourhood; \texttt{OPTIMAL} in a full primary search refers only to the declared cube. Neither is automatically an unrestricted lattice proof. Restarted solves do not retain a previous solve's learned clauses.

\subsection{Project Attention-DQN Baseline}\label{sec:lstma_approach}
The bundled project reference uses an explicit $(n,5)$ observation: coordinates, residue type and normalised chain index, with sentinels for unplaced residues. A duelling transformer estimates five relative placement actions. Training retains the reference Double-DQN target, prioritised replay, Adam optimiser, FP32 precision, valid-action mask and at most one replay update per episode. The behaviour-action mask also retains its reference symmetry restrictions and one-step trap lookahead. The Double-DQN bootstrap chooses the next policy action with an unmasked argmax; this inherited limitation can select an action excluded by the environment mask. Its contribution to the observed learning outcomes has not been isolated. Separate models are initialised for each sequence and seed. This source is associated with the attention-based RL comparison~\cite{AttnDRL2504}, but the frozen project configurations in Table~\ref{tab:cluster_configs} are not silently substituted for the published protocol.

The exploration schedule is fixed over 200,000 training episodes even when an elapsed-time cutoff truncates training. Periodic greedy evaluation restores the previous training mode in a \texttt{finally} block. If 200,000 episodes finish before the elapsed budget, training stops and the frozen policy continues sampling with exploration probability 0.05; those samples and their costs are identified separately. The old MPS execution backend is not used in the CPU campaign.

\section{Completed CPU Experiment}\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Hardware, Software and Allocation}
Runs used Apocrita's homogeneous EHC CPU pool, whose nodes contain AMD EPYC 9965 processors~\cite{ApocritaEHC}. Each run requested eight CPU cores and 32 GiB of RAM. Disposable resource pilots screened thread counts 1, 2, 4 and 8 on three representative configurations; eight was the fastest tested RL setting and was retained as the common allocation. This selection does not establish optimal scaling beyond eight cores. No GPU backend was used. The frozen environment specified Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.9.0, NumPy 2.3.4 and OR-Tools 9.15.6755. Task metadata record the host, CPU model, dependency versions, allocated cores, seeds, exact sequence strings and executed-source hashes.

\subsection{Elapsed Budgets and Stopping Rules}
The common scientific budget was 24 elapsed hours per sequence, seed and method, including model initialisation, RL training, search, periodic evaluation and in-run output work. CP-SAT could finish earlier only when it proved optimality for the full declared cube. A proof permits carrying its certified incumbent forward to later reporting thresholds; this is not additional computation. Post-cutoff checkpoint saving may add seconds to Slurm elapsed time and does not extend the score-acquisition budget.

Jobs requested Apocrita's ten-day scheduler ceiling while application guards imposed the shorter scientific cutoff~\cite{ApocritaRuntime}. The inherited absolute campaign deadline was 16 October 2026 at 20:38:37 UTC, including queue time. It was not reset for the continuation. All requested observations acquired complete budget coverage or an applicable proof before that deadline. At most 24 array allocations ran concurrently. The 74 newly submitted tasks in array \texttt{30988549} all completed with exit code \texttt{0:0}; these statuses alone are not solver optimality certificates.

\subsection{Design and Reuse Provenance}
Eight strings of lengths 20, 24, 25, 36, 46, 48, 50 and 58 were evaluated with seeds 0--4 for both CP-SAT and RL, yielding 80 observations. Six seed-0 CP-SAT/RL runs on 3d4, 3d6 and 3d8 were reused from the completed feasibility campaign under unchanged configurations, numerical source, software versions, CPU model, allocation and compute budget. The continuation submitted the remaining 74 tasks. Manifest and artifact hashes identify imported observations, and no result is counted twice.

The six feasibility results were seen before the two-method campaign scope was selected. The full sample is therefore not described as entirely unseen confirmatory data. Fixed-hybrid feasibility observations were not added to the primary method means. Each RL observation initialised an independent sequence-specific model; no trained model was transferred between sequences.

\subsection{Endpoints and Verification}
The primary endpoint is the best independently validated complete-fold contact count witnessed at or before the elapsed cutoff. We report one-, two-, twelve- and twenty-four-hour trajectories. Final aggregation requires full requested elapsed-budget coverage or an exact proof for the full declared cube; missing folds and partial runs are not assigned zero. Final tables show means and sample standard deviations over five seeds. Seed-wise paired differences are descriptive, without a claim of population-wide statistical significance.

The cluster analyser reported 363,488 recorded witness checks and zero verification errors across the 80 observations. This number counts checked witness records, not necessarily distinct conformations. Independent validation establishes complete-fold geometry and contact counts; optimality is assessed separately from full-search solver status and objective bounds. Greedy policy evaluations, training episodes and update counts are separate endpoints. Empty evaluations caused by the deadline are excluded from policy summaries, while genuinely zero-contact completed folds remain zero.

\subsection{Context from the Published RL Study}
Liu and Iba's attention-RL study reports best scores of 11, 13, 9, 18, 29 and 29 on the exact strings corresponding to our 3d1, 3d2, 3d3, 3d4, 3d6 and 3d7 respectively~\cite{AttnDRL2504}. Our 46- and 58-residue strings are not included in that study. The original experiments use RTX 3090 Ti/A100 GPUs and training schedules of 80,000--400,000 episodes. Our saved project configurations differ from the published protocol, and our main endpoint is a mean over five cold-start CPU runs, rather than a published best score. The reported values therefore provide context, not a matched elapsed-budget replication. In particular, improved CP-SAT contact counts on the shared 48- and 50-residue strings do not establish an equal-time advantage over the published GPU implementation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
Under the tested allocation, direct CP-SAT search provided stronger best-fold quality on six sequences, while both approaches attained the optima on the two shortest. The one-hour versus twenty-four-hour paired comparison shows that this result is not solely an end-of-budget effect. It supports CP-SAT as a practical method for these cold-start CPU workloads. It does not establish that longer CP-SAT computation will close its remaining optimality gaps.

The CPU budget comparison is not a converged-policy comparison. Large replay batches and sequence-specific networks made the longer RL workloads expensive, and most of their training schedule remained unfinished. The preserved reference's unmasked bootstrap argmax is a possible source of target bias, but no controlled variant was run to isolate its effect. A corrected target, different schedule, smaller architecture or random valid-action search would each require a separately declared comparison. GPU acceleration and reuse of a policy across many sequences could change the cost balance; neither was tested here.

A prospective independent scientific-machine-learning (SciML) arm could optimise a continuous relaxation by gradient flow, then decode candidate lattice folds and submit them to the same independent checker. Such a study would need a frozen construction rule, fresh seeds, the same CPU and elapsed budgets, and explicit accounting for training, initialisation and decoding. No SciML experiment was performed here, and no CP-SAT--SciML hybrid result is claimed.

The evidence is limited to eight fixed strings and five seeds per method. Six feasibility outcomes were seen before selecting the two-method continuation. Solver seed variation under eight workers does not imply deterministic repeated execution. The baseline is a project configuration, with a proposed architecture for 3d8, rather than an exact reproduction of every original-paper setting. No matched random-search control or specialised CPSP/H-core baseline was included~\cite{CPSP2009}. The result therefore cannot establish the frontier of HP solving or the general superiority of constraint programming over reinforcement learning.

The cluster analyser reported zero errors on all checked witness records, but this manuscript revision relies on the supplied remote report and has not independently reloaded all remote artifacts locally. The geometry checks do not establish learning quality, and longer-sequence CP-SAT bounds do not establish optimality. The HP lattice task remains a combinatorial model rather than realistic all-atom protein folding.

\section{Conclusion}
The completed eight-sequence, five-seed comparison found that CP-SAT and the project attention-DQN baseline tied on the two shortest sequences, while CP-SAT obtained better best-fold quality in every paired seed on the other six. On those six strings, its one-hour incumbents already exceeded the RL twenty-four-hour incumbents. CP-SAT certified declared-cube optimality on the first three sequences; no optimality proof was obtained on the remaining five. These findings establish practical performance under the specified cold-start CPU budget. Future work should isolate baseline learning limitations, measure training amortisation and evaluate learned solver cooperation in a separate controlled experiment.

\section{Separate Exploratory Hybrid Evidence}\label{sec:exploratory}
\subsection{Fixed RL--CP-SAT Feasibility}
The earlier seed-0 feasibility pass tested CP-SAT, RL and a fixed hybrid on 3d4, 3d6 and 3d8. The hybrid used the same DQN with a bounded CP repair every 1,000 episodes once a valid incumbent existed. Ten movable residues alternated between suffix and central windows, with a 30-second repair limit charged to the elapsed budget. CP folds improved the search incumbent but were not added to RL replay. Its best contact counts were 14, 22 and 29 respectively, compared with RL's 10, 16 and 17 and CP-SAT's 18, 31 and 41. These one-seed outcomes motivated follow-up questions; they are not five-seed hybrid evidence and do not evaluate a learned repair controller.

\subsection{DecisionBoost Prototype and Future Replication}
A separate local DecisionBoost prototype combines a frozen ModernBERT-large sequence encoder, a small transformer fold constructor, a learned repair-region controller and bounded CP-SAT feedback. It selects suffix/internal repair windows, with verified solver improvements supplying learning targets. A completed local pilot reported five pipelines and 72 held-out sequences per pipeline. Learned repair selection improved mean final contacts by approximately 0.61 relative to training-selected static repair and 1.11 relative to fixed ten-residue suffix repair under its tested call caps. The reported sequence-group bootstrap interval for the former difference was approximately 0.38--0.87 contacts, conditional on that test set.

These are exploratory local findings, not results of the completed primary CPU campaign. The pilot had no matched RL arm and did not establish end-to-end equal-time superiority. Its proposed fresh-sequence cluster replication remains unexecuted and separate. Equal solver call caps do not imply equal elapsed costs: encoder loading, solver teaching, neural training, proposal inference and online repair must be charged and reported. Encoder pretraining cost is unknown. This manuscript makes no novelty, convergence or learned-hybrid superiority claim from that prototype.
